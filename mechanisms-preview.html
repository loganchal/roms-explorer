The transformation

01

Tracer tendencies

The model records the temperature and salinity tendencies associated with each physical process, j.

Ṫj(z),   Ṡj(z)

02

Density tendencies

Thermal expansion and saline contraction convert each process’s tracer tendencies to a density tendency.

ρ̇j = ρ0(−αṪj + βṠj)

03

Stratification tendencies

Each density tendency contributes to the change in potential energy anomaly over the selected layer.

φ̇j = gH ∫−H0 (ρ̄̇j − ρ̇j) z dz

04

Accumulated contributions

Integrating each process through time connects the initial stratification to its subsequent development.

φ(t) = φ(t0) + Σj ∫ φ̇j dt + C

About this example and budget closure

This is one preselected offshore column and one spring–summer season. The process contributions come from diagnostics evaluated online in the model, rather than a reconstruction from daily-mean temperature and salinity. The T and S profiles in the first view are daily-mean snapshots for context. Potential density is calculated from those snapshots using the model’s nonlinear equation of state at surface pressure and displayed as σθ (potential density minus 1000 kg m⁻³).

The layer extends from the surface to 200 m. Here H is its thickness, ρ̄ is its mean potential density, and C collects equation-of-state, moving-grid and tracer-update corrections. The seasonal change shown is relative to 1 September, not a climatological anomaly.
